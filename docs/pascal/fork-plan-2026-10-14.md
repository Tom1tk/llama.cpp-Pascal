# A Pascal-tuned llama.cpp fork — plan

**2026-10-14 · plan only, no runs · follow-up to [`prefill-ttft-2026-08-25.md`](https://github.com/Tom1tk/p100-inference-benchmarks/blob/main/Research/prefill-ttft-2026-08-25.md) and [`chunked-gdn-2026-08-25.md`](https://github.com/Tom1tk/p100-inference-benchmarks/blob/main/Research/chunked-gdn-2026-08-25.md)**

This repo holds the fork's code and planning. Every measurement, run log and hypothesis lives in the benchmark repo, [Tom1tk/p100-inference-benchmarks](https://github.com/Tom1tk/p100-inference-benchmarks/), and its rules apply to every run made from this plan.

Goal: get the most out of the P100 for Qwen3.8-27B-class hybrid models, on **two cards (main target) and one card (secondary target)**. Prefill first, then decode, then server behaviour, with quality held at baseline.

**Single-card target.** Every kernel change must also be measured on one P100, and the single card gets the same Phase 0 profile. The single-card arm uses the model the benchmark repo designates for it, `Qwen3.8-27B-UD-IQ3_S` with `-sm none` (reference point: the Saluki IQ2-mix one-card MTP run gave 17.45 t/s decode and 179 t/s prefill at 16k). A change that helps the pair but slows one card down does not ship as a default. Items that only matter with two cards are marked **2-card only** below.

Each work item below is gated by a measurement, so engineering time goes only
where the profile says the time goes. [RUNBOOK](https://github.com/Tom1tk/p100-inference-benchmarks/blob/main/RUNBOOK.md) §2.1 (run economy) applies to
every step.

## 0. Headline findings from this research pass

1. **The repo's "prefill is ~85% non-GEMM" (H17 → H18) conflicts with the FLOP count.**
   - The model is about 54 GFLOP per token in weight GEMMs (2 × ~27B params).
   - At `-ub 512`, 222.7 t/s works out to 6.0 TFLOPS per card. At `-ub 2048`, 310.5 t/s works out to 8.4 TFLOPS per card, which is 45% of the 18.7 TFLOPS FP16 peak.
   - If GEMMs were only 15% of the time, they would have to run at 40–56 TFLOPS per card: 2–3× faster than the hardware can go.
   - H17's arithmetic assumed FP32 cuBLAS is exactly 2× slower than FP16. It probably isn't on P100: cuBLAS HGEMM on GP100 reaches a lower fraction of its peak than SGEMM does.
   - If the achieved FP16:FP32 ratio is about 1.3×, the same −13% result gives **GEMMs at ~50% of prefill time**.
   - **Working estimate: GEMMs are 50–60% of prefill at `-ub 2048`.** Phase 0 replaces this with a measurement.
   - **Measured (Phase 0, 2026-10-14): GEMMs are ~87% of one card's prefill op time, at 12–13 TFLOPS (~67% of FP16 peak).** Both H18 and the estimate above were wrong. See §2.

2. **Upstream now has a chunked GDN CUDA kernel, and it barely helps a 27B dense model.**
   - [#29353](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/29353) (am17an) supersedes [#26001](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/26001). It was approved by ggerganov on 2026-10-13 and is not merged yet.
   - It uses BF16 tensor-core MMA and is enabled only on sm_86/sm_89, so **Pascal falls back to the per-token kernel**.
   - Its measured gain on Qwen3.8-27B is **+0.9% (3090), +3.1% (4090) and +6.4% (RTX PRO 6000)**. The MoE 35B-A3B gains more (+7–21%), because GDN is a larger share of a sparse model.
   - The ZINC analysis profiled the scan at ~10% of SSM time, with the GEMMs before the scan at 60–70% ([source](https://zolotukhin.ai/zinc/docs/delta-net-llamacpp-analysis/)).
   - **This demotes the chunked GDN kernel from "largest lever" to "probably 5–15% on Pascal".** The scan's share may be larger here, because Pascal has no tensor cores to make the GEMMs cheap, but that needs measuring.
   - **Measured: the GDN scan is ~6% of prefill and ~2% of decode on one card.** So P4's ceiling is ~6%.

3. **Tensor-split communication is serialised with compute and copied in F32.**
   - `ggml-backend-meta.cpp:2410–2445` runs subgraph *i*, then the AllReduce, then subgraph *i+1*. On Pascal only the butterfly fallback runs (H10).
   - The butterfly copies each card's full partial result to the other card (`ggml_backend_tensor_copy_async`, which goes through the host when P2P is off) and then adds.
   - Estimate per prefill ubatch:
     - 2048 tok × 5120 hidden × 4 B = 42 MB per reduce.
     - About 2 reduces per layer × 65 layers ≈ 5.5 GB each way per card.
     - At the ~1 GB/s effective rate seen on a similar dual-2080 Ti rig ([source](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/pcie-allreduce-dual-2080ti)), comms cost **0.5–1.0 s of a 6.6 s ubatch (8–15%)** with the GPUs idle the whole time.
   - Precedent: the SYCL backend's BF16-compressed AllReduce gained **+20% prefill** for tensor split on dual Arc ([commit](https://app.semanticdiff.com/gh/ggml-org/llama.cpp/commit/e9fb3b3fc0300501179b0ce61e907be767e8c859)).

4. **Decode has roughly 2–3× theoretical headroom, and two Pascal-specific handicaps.**
   - Bandwidth ceiling: each card streams ~8.3 GB of Q4_K_M per token. At ~550 GB/s practical, that's ~15 ms, or **~65 t/s**.
   - Measured drafter-free decode is 20.3 t/s, which is 49 ms per token. **About 34 ms per token is overhead, not weight streaming.**
   - Suspects:
     - **`dp4a` is emulated on sm_60** (`common.cuh:736`: four scalar int8 multiply-adds). MMVQ, the quantized matvec used for decode, may be ALU-bound rather than bandwidth-bound on P100. This is the same problem the gfx906 forks attacked.
     - **CUDA graphs are disabled for cc < Volta** (`ggml-cuda.cu:4235`). Each token issues ~2,000+ kernel launches with no graph replay. The gate dates from the original graphs PR ([#6766](https://app.semanticdiff.com/gh/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/6766/overview)) and was a performance judgement, not a correctness one. Volta and Turing were re-enabled later in #25749. **Pascal was never re-tested.**
     - **~130 AllReduce round trips per token** through host-staged copies. Latency-bound, not bandwidth-bound (same 2080 Ti source).

5. **The closest analogue is the AMD gfx906 (MI50) fork community.** That hardware also lacks matrix cores and dp4a-class shortcuts. [iacopPBK/llama.cpp-gfx906](https://github.com/iacopPBK/llama.cpp-gfx906) keeps its arch kernels in `ggml/src/ggml-cuda/gfx906/` with small dispatch hooks: warp-cooperative MMVQ, a Q8 flash-attention kernel, fused norms, and software-pipelined loads. Descendants include [mx-llama.cpp](https://hub.docker.com/r/mxxm/mx-llama.cpp) and [gfx-906-turbo](https://github.com/arte-fact/llamacpp-gfx-906-turbo). **Copy the structure. The ISA-specific code does not port.**

## 1. Choosing the base

| Candidate | For | Against | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ggml-org/llama.cpp mainline** | Has `-sm tensor` (the meta backend), `qwen35`, MTP, DFlash2, and the chunked-GDN reference coming in #29353. Biggest contributor base, so our generic fixes can go upstream | Moves fast, so rebase cost is real | **Base** |
| `dflash2-rebased` (our current tree, `57affa09`) | Already built and characterised. Holds the H17 and H22 patches | A local working copy on an older upstream | Starting point only. Re-fork from current upstream and cherry-pick |
| ik_llama.cpp | Strong CPU and fused-op work | No tensor split (`-sm graph` only), lost Phase 1, no `qwen35` prefill work (H12) | Idea donor only |
| buun / turboquant | Carries the sm_60 FP16 correctness fix | Only exclusive feature is TurboQuant (closed) | Idea donor only |
| gfx906 forks | Right *problem*, right *structure* | AMD ISA (DPP, `v_dot`); some are stale | Structural template |
| vLLM / SGLang / TRT-LLM / ExLlamaV3 | Best-in-class *ideas*: comm overlap, custom AllReduce, prefix caching for hybrid models | Need sm_70 or sm_80 at minimum; no path to sm_60 | Read, don't run |

**Fork layout** (the gfx906 pattern):

- `ggml/src/ggml-cuda/pascal/`: every new kernel lives here.
- Upstream files get only small dispatch hooks, each behind `GGML_CUDA_PASCAL_<FEATURE>` (env var, default **off** until it passes its gate, then default on for cc 600).
- Keep a patch-queue mindset: one feature per commit, rebased monthly onto an upstream tag. Anything not Pascal-specific (an F16 AllReduce, graphs on sm_60 if they win) is offered upstream to cut rebase load. Upstream's `AGENTS.md` bans AI-written PR descriptions, commit messages and automated submissions, so upstream PRs are written and submitted by hand.
- Builds go in `build-cuda-p100/` with `-DCMAKE_CUDA_ARCHITECTURES=60 -DLLAMA_BUILD_TESTS=ON`.

**Quality gate for every kernel change** (quality is the binding constraint, see the benchmark [README](https://github.com/Tom1tk/p100-inference-benchmarks/blob/main/README.md)):

- KLD against the upstream build on a fixed text, and perplexity on wikitext-2.
- Thresholds: mean KLD < 0.001 and top-1 agreement ≥ 99%.
- #26001 was rejected at KLD 0.0083 with 96.8% top-1. That is the bar *not* to repeat.
- Use the pflash tree's `llama-niah` binary for long-context changes.

**P2 scoping (2026-10-14).** Overlap needs a second token stream to compute while one
reduces: subgraph *i+1* consumes the reduce of subgraph *i*. Within one ubatch nothing can
overlap. The working route is interleaving two consecutive ubatches by subgraph (valid:
ubatch 2's layer *i* needs only ubatch 1's KV/state for layer *i*). That changes the
llama decode loop, not just the meta backend, so P2 waits until P1's measured gain (H34)
shows how much comms time is left.

**P3 first result (H33, 2026-10-14).** The cuBLAS algorithm sweep found the lever without a
custom kernel. At n=2048 `CUBLAS_GEMM_ALGO3` beats the default by 3–11% at the 2-card
shapes and up to 25% at the 1-card ones, and reaches 16.5–16.9 TFLOPS (~90% of FP16 peak).
At n=512–1024 the default pick collapses to 5–7 TFLOPS and ALGO3 is 1.7–2.7× faster. ALGO3
loses below n=256. Landed as `GGML_CUDA_PASCAL_GEMM_ALGO=1` (n ≥ 512 only), end-to-end in H34.
With HGEMM near peak, a custom SIMT GEMM is off the table; what's left in P3 is the
dequant-to-F16 pass (6–7%) and the F16↔F32 activation conversions.

**P1 result (H34, 2026-10-14).** F16 reduce lifts 2-card prefill **+6.8%** at 16k (337 → 361
t/s), decode flat. KLD vs off is ≈ 0 with 100% top-1, because both cards round their own
partial too. Adopted. Halving the bytes recovered ~7 of the 18% comms idle, so **~9–11% is
left, and P2's gate (> 5%) is open**. Same run: Q6_K in mmvh lifts 2-card decode +5.2%. ALGO3
failed a KLD gate that compared two FP16 orders with each other, so it is re-gated against
the FP32 reference in H35.

**ALGO3 result (H35, 2026-10-14).** ALGO3 vs the Q5 FP32 reference: mean KLD 0.0619 vs 0.0624
for the default GEMM, so it rounds differently but loses nothing. **Adopted.** 2-card prefill
+3.1% on top of F16 reduce (371.8 t/s at 16k; +8.6% with P1). 1-card only +1.0%: FFN gate/up
gains nothing at 17408×5120, and the card likely sits at the 175 W cap.

**P2 design, revised: chunk the reducing GEMM by tokens (no llama-loop change).**
Each meta subgraph ends in the node that gets reduced (an out-projection or FFN-down GEMM,
or the delayed partial sum after it). Split that last GEMM along tokens (ne11) into K=2 views.
Compute chunk A, then start A's reduce (F16 convert + peer copy) while chunk B computes.
- Needs a **second CUDA stream for the copy**. Today `cpy_tensor_async` copies on the *source*
  compute stream, so chunk B would queue behind A's copy. The fork path records an event on
  compute, the side stream waits, copies, and records the event the destination's ADD waits on.
  The source's next write into the shared F16 tmp buffer must also wait on that copy event.
- Chunks run the GEMM at n=1024, where the default cuBLAS pick collapses (H33) and ALGO3
  holds, so **P2 requires ALGO3**. That is now adopted.
- Ceiling: hide ≤ half of each reduce. ~9–11% of prefill is still comms after P1, so
  **+4–5% at best**, minus ~1% for chunk launches. FFN-down chunk compute (~5.7 ms at n=1024) covers
  its copy (~4 ms). The attention out-proj chunk (~2 ms) covers about half of its copy.
- Step 0 before building: one nsys trace of pp2048 with P1+ALGO3 on, to confirm the comms
  share left. Skip the build if it is under 5% (P2's gate).

**P2 step 0 result (2026-10-14, bench a16a6c2).** pp2048 with P1 + ALGO3 + mmvh is 4826 ms
of wall (Phase 0.2: 5275), and each card idles 10.9% of it, so the gate is met. memcpy dropped
from 919 to 260 ms. All of the idle sits in the reduce: convert → copy → empty gap → add. The
empty half is not compute waiting on comms. **The two copy directions are serialised:** 0→1
(21 MB, 2.06 ms) and then 1→0, starting 9 µs after the first ends. That is issue order, not
PCIe. `cpy_tensor_async` puts card 1's wait on the 0→1 event *before* card 1 queues its own
1→0 copy. A cheaper fix comes before the chunked GEMM: `GGML_PASCAL_DUPLEX_REDUCE=1` defers
the destination wait to that stream's next graph, sync or transfer, so both copies run at once
on full-duplex PCIe. Ceiling ≈ 5.4% at pp2048, with no GEMM split. Bench H36. The
chunked-GEMM overlap stays parked until H36 shows how much idle is left.

## 5b. KV-cache compression (TurboQuant question)

**Status upstream (2026-10-14).** TurboQuant is not in mainline and nothing is in flight. The CPU PR [#21089](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/21089) was closed unmerged on 2026-06-03, the feature request [#20977](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/20977) was closed on 2026-08-20, and every later TurboQuant / TQ3 / E8 KV-type PR was closed too. Maintainers' view in #20977: the gain over mainline's q4_0 KV is small for the speed and upkeep cost. What mainline did take is TurboQuant's main ingredient: [#21038](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/21038) (merged 2026-04-01) applies a Walsh-Hadamard rotation to K and V automatically whenever the KV type is quantized (`src/llama-kv-cache.cpp:325`, disable with `LLAMA_ATTN_ROT_DISABLE=1`). So mainline `q8_0`/`q4_0` KV is already "rotate then round to nearest". The research thread to watch is [#24139](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/24139) (KVarN: rotation plus variance normalization, aimed at error build-up during long decode).

**Size of the prize on this model.** Only 16 of 65 layers have a KV cache (`full_attention_interval` 4), with 4 KV heads of dim 256. f16 KV is **64 KiB per token**: 1 GiB at 16k, **6.1 GiB at 100k** (about 3 GiB per card under tensor split). At 100k, q8_0 is about 3.3 GiB, q4_0 about 1.7 GiB and a 3-bit codec about 1.3 GiB. The GDN state is fixed-size and not affected. This also explains H4: turbo3 saving 788 MiB at 16k is the correct size, not a sign that turbo was only partly applied.

**What compression can and cannot buy here.**
- Decode: at 100k the KV is about 27% of the bytes each card reads per token, so a 4-bit KV that costs no extra ALU could give up to about +20-25% decode at 100k, and about +5% at 16k. On P100 it currently costs ALU instead (no dp4a, dequantize in the FA inner loop): measured -1.5% for q8_0 (H25) and -14.3% for turbo3 (H4) at 16k.
- Prefill: no direct gain. Prefill is compute-bound and quantized KV adds dequantize work. Indirect gains only: freed VRAM allows a larger `-ub` or more context.
- VRAM: the real win, mostly for the single card. H25 found MTP does not fit on one card with IQ3_S; 1-3 GB freed at long context may change that.
- Quality: "no effect at all" is not reachable below 8 bits; the target is the same KLD gate as every kernel (mean KLD < 0.001, top-1 >= 99%), plus a long-context retrieval check (NIAH with distractors), because the #20977 thread reports TurboQuant losing multi-key retrieval while perplexity looks fine.

|---|---|---|
| K1 | **Speed side already settled (H25: q8_0 −1.5% decode, q4_0 ≈ q8_0; 100k serves on q8_0). Quality side (KLD, NIAH) folded into the Phase 8 quality gate 2026-10-14, no separate run.** Original: **Measure first, no code.** Mainline `q8_0` and `q4_0` KV (rotation on) vs f16 at 16k and 64k: decode, prefill, VRAM, KLD, NIAH. ## 6. Order of work

```
Phase 0 (measure)  ──►  D5 flag (+3.6%), D1 graphs (closed: no gain)
                   ──►  S1 / H19 (config only — biggest practical win)
                   ──►  K1 KV-cache measurement (config only)
                   ──►  D2 (decode, both configs) · P1+P2 (2-card prefill, 18% serialised comms)
                   ──►  P3 (≤ +15–20% of GEMM time) · P5 only if 100k TTFT is the goal
                   ──►  P4 (≤ ~6%) after #29353 merges
```

Each item gets an H-number in the benchmark repo's [HYPOTHESES.md](https://github.com/Tom1tk/p100-inference-benchmarks/blob/main/HYPOTHESES.md) before it's built, with a pre-registered prediction,
as H17 did. Each patch lands with its microbench, its A/B run labels (2-card and 1-card) and its KLD result.

## 7. What not to do

- Anything that needs tensor cores, `dp4a`, BF16 or FP8 hardware, or Triton (FLA kernels, FlashQLA, Marlin, vLLM proper).
- Porting vLLM or SGLang wholesale. Their wins are ideas (comm overlap, custom AllReduce, hybrid prefix cache), and all of those fit in llama.cpp.
- A from-scratch engine (tinygrad, TVM). See [`prefill-ttft-2026-08-25.md`](https://github.com/Tom1tk/p100-inference-benchmarks/blob/main/Research/prefill-ttft-2026-08-25.md) §5.4.
- A chunked GDN kernel *before* Phase 0. Upstream's data says it may be worth little on a dense 27B.

## 8. Last in the queue

- **K3, a 3-bit TurboQuant-style KV codec.** Deprioritised by user call (2026-10-14). Mainline's rotation plus `q8_0`/`q4_0` KV and the K2 Pascal kernel come first. Kept here for the record:

Must pass KLD and NIAH-with-distractors |
